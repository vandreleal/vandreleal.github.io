Encoding Pricing Rules Without Forking the UI

July 15, 2025 · 2 min read

Rules in components scale by branching. Rules in data scale by extension. Knowing which side of that line a thing belongs on is the call that keeps a pricing UI from rotting as new products land.

The marketing site I worked on rendered pricing for several products, and each one priced differently. One was usage-based with multiple input dimensions and multipliers. Another was tiered by stored data, scaled by monthly active users. Another mixed time-based units with storage tiers and addons that only applied on certain plans. New products kept arriving with new billing models.

The UI had already been solved in an earlier round of work: range sliders, comparison tables, plan cards, mobile-friendly grids. What kept breaking was the math behind the sliders. Every time a billing rule changed, the temptation was to add another conditional in the component. Two products in, that approach was already starting to rot.

Plans as data

I moved the rules out of components and into the plan definition. Each plan declared what kind of calculation it used and what inputs it accepted. The calculation logic itself lived in shared utility functions, one per billing model. The component became generic: it read the plan, rendered the relevant inputs, and called the right utility.

Adding a new billing dimension stopped being a UI change. A new tier, a new multiplier, an addon that only applied above a usage threshold: each one was a small extension to the schema and a small new utility. The components did not notice.

The legacy corner

Pricing carries history. Customers on older plans need to see prices that match what they signed up under, even when the public-facing rules have moved on. The schema accommodates these as named legacy plans that map to frozen rule sets. New products launch in the main schema; legacy plans live in their own corner of it.

It is not the cleanest separation, but the discipline is the same one: rules live with the plan, never with the component.

What I learned

The first time you find yourself writing a slug check inside a pricing component, the schema is wrong. Pricing rules belong with the plan, not in the layer that draws the plan. Once that boundary is drawn, every new product slots into the schema without touching the UI.
